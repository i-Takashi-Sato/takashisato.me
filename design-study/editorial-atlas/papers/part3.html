MECHANISM / SCHEMATIC

01 / RESEARCH QUESTION

What must survive a shutdown?

Detection alone is not governance. A system may stop while affected people, services and unresolved decisions remain exposed.

Part III distinguishes a reversible Circuit Breaker from accountable retirement, and insists that the ending preserve continuity, evidence, remedy and the practical capacity to decide.

02 / THE PROTOCOL

Nine evidence-producing functions.

  1. 01 / AuthorizeRecord scope, decision maker and review path.
  2. 02 / ContainEmploy tested fallback or a separately authorized safe-stop.
  3. 03 / PreserveKeep versions, cases, logs and evidence under lawful control.
  4. 04 / NotifyProvide accurate notice appropriate to rights and risks.
  5. 05 / RemedyLocate affected decisions and preserve meaningful review and relief.
  6. 06 / TransferAssign decisions and outstanding obligations to accountable successors.
  7. 07 / DecommissionDisable integrations and verify unauthorized use has ceased.
  8. 08 / AssureIndependently verify continuity, remedy and security.
  9. 09 / Learn & closeRecord lessons, follow-up owners and verified closure.

These functions are dependency-constrained. Parallel work is possible when prerequisites permit; the sequence is not a simplistic “nine clicks” shutdown UI.

03 / AUTHORITY RETURN

Ending software is not returning authority.

Power may remain embedded in defaults, data definitions, access rules and queue architecture long after a system is deactivated.

The successor needs genuine decision capacity, resources and responsibility for the obligations that remain.

04 / BOUNDED VERIFICATION

Explore a model, not a claim of field success.

8,564Reachable states explored within the declared model

The finite-state checks support internal consistency claims only in their specified abstraction.

05 / CLAIM LIMIT

An ending must not erase obligations.

The protocol is not an operating approval, legal certification or demonstrated real-world outcome. The primary paper documents its definitions and the precise limits of testing.
