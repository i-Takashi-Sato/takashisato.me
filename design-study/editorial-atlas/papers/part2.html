MECHANISM / SCHEMATIC

01 / RESEARCH QUESTION

Can procedure continue after capacity declines?

An institution may document its decisions properly while losing its practical capacity to judge, correct and govern them.

Part II introduces Procedurally Masked Governing-Capacity Loss (PMGCL): a descriptive joint state, not a presumption of maliciousness. Pre-Abuse Collapse (PAC) is treated as a provisional explanatory subtype requiring an independently established causal boundary.

02 / THE FOUR OBJECTS

Procedure is not judgment.

P / PROCEDURE

Procedural fidelity

Visible steps, documentation and recorded completion.

J / JUDGMENT

Judgment contribution

Independent evidence and reasoning beyond model output.

G / AUTHORITY

Governing capacity

Real ability to intervene, resource, remedy or stop.

N / FUNCTION

Protected function

An independently measured, predeclared function—not merely model accuracy.

Declining judgment and capacity alone indicate risk. The stronger PMGCL description also requires protected-function impairment. Attribution to PAC requires separate evidence of the causal boundary.

03 / CANDIDATE MECHANISMS

Ordinary operation can hide deterioration.

The paper considers cognitive externalization, legibility capture, responsibility inversion and voice attenuation as provisional mechanisms. This is not a closed taxonomy of institutional failure.

04 / FALSIFIABILITY

Five propositions, open to rejection.

The paper formulates five rejectable propositions and counterevidence conditions. The precise contrasts and qualifying observations belong in the versioned working paper, not in a decorative simulated dashboard.

05 / CLAIM LIMIT

Not a predictive score.

A descriptive model cannot establish that a particular institution is failing from procedure alone. Evidence, declared comparisons, protected-function measurements and causal identification must remain distinguishable.
